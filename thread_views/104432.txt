TITLE: logging: swo: drain hardware pipeline in panic handler

*--- opening post by JayToltTech (2026-02-23 23:17:53+00:00)
The SWO log backend's `panic()` handler is a no-op. When `LOG_PANIC()`
is called (e.g., before a bootloader jumps to an app, or during a fatal
error), the logging subsystem flushes software buffers to the ITM FIFO,
but the TPIU hardware pipeline still has bytes in-flight on the SWO pin.
The debug probe loses these final messages.

This patch:
1. Spins on `ITM_TCR.BUSY` until the ITM formatter is idle
2. Busy-waits for the TPIU output stage to clock remaining bytes out
   at the configured SWO baud rate

The drain time is estimated from `CONFIG_LOG_BACKEND_SWO_FREQ_HZ`
(~200 bytes × 10 bits/byte with 50% margin). When the frequency is
left at 0 (default = max supported), a conservative 2 ms fallback
is used.

Discovered while working on MCUboot, where SWO logs were lost during
the transition from bootloader to app.

Tested on nRF5340 with J-Link SWO viewer.

*--- Copilot (2026-02-23 23:22:33+00:00)
The calculation could result in a long wait time if CONFIG_LOG_BACKEND_SWO_FREQ_HZ is configured to a very low value. For example, at 100 kHz (a low but potentially valid frequency), this would wait for 30 milliseconds; at 10 kHz it would be 300 milliseconds. While this is acceptable in a panic scenario where thoroughness is more important than speed, consider documenting the expected range of wait times or adding a sanity check/cap to prevent unexpectedly long delays if the frequency is misconfigured.
```suggestion
 *
 * To avoid excessively long busy-wait delays when the SWO frequency is
 * misconfigured to a very low value, cap the drain wait to a reasonable
 * upper bound.
 */
#define SWO_DRAIN_WAIT_MAX_US 50000UL

#if CONFIG_LOG_BACKEND_SWO_FREQ_HZ > 0
#define SWO_DRAIN_WAIT_US_RAW \
	((200UL * 10 * 1500000) / CONFIG_LOG_BACKEND_SWO_FREQ_HZ)
#if SWO_DRAIN_WAIT_US_RAW > SWO_DRAIN_WAIT_MAX_US
#define SWO_DRAIN_WAIT_US SWO_DRAIN_WAIT_MAX_US
#else
#define SWO_DRAIN_WAIT_US SWO_DRAIN_WAIT_US_RAW
#endif
#else
```
